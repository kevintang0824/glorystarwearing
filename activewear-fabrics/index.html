Activewear Fabric Selection

A useful activewear fabric specification records fiber content, weight, construction, stretch and recovery, surface finish, color and the checks required for the intended garment. Compare candidates against the same written requirements, then record the approved fabric and finished sample.

Six fields in an activewear fabric brief

Do not ask for the “best” fabric without defining best for whom and for what activity. These fields make alternatives easier to compare.

1. Product and use

Name the garment, wearer, climate and intended activity. A legging, sports bra and casual top may prioritize different properties.

2. Composition direction

Record the fiber-content target or acceptable range, but do not treat composition alone as proof of softness, recovery or durability.

3. Weight and construction

Record fabric weight and whether the material is knitted, ribbed, brushed or otherwise constructed as required by the design.

4. Stretch and recovery

Define required stretch directions and how recovery will be evaluated after movement or wear simulation.

5. Feel and appearance

Describe surface, softness, compression direction, sheen, texture and drape using references where possible.

6. Coverage and color

Name the intended colors and how coverage will be checked under stretch, lighting and the selected garment construction.

Fabric choices depend on the product

Use the same vocabulary across the collection, then adjust priorities for the fit and construction of each category.

A three-stage approval path

The fabric should stay traceable from early comparison to final garment approval. Record reference codes and changes at every stage.

1. Compare specifications

Review composition, weight, construction, available colors and any evidence required for stated performance or compliance.

2. Review the swatch

Compare hand feel, surface, stretch direction, recovery and color against the written brief. A swatch does not replace garment testing.

3. Approve the garment sample

Evaluate the intended color, size and construction during the intended activity, then record the approved fabric reference with the final specification.

Turn fabric claims into review criteria

Write down what the buyer expects to observe and what evidence is needed. The following prompts help define a sample review; they are not test results or performance claims for a Glory Star Wear product.

Keep composition, weight and color in the comparison even when the main concern is feel or performance. Use the same approval record for the quality-control plan.

A fabric brief you can copy into an inquiry

Fill in confirmed details and mark the rest “to review.” A reference garment or swatch can accompany this brief when a description alone is difficult.

  • Product and use: garment category, wearer, activity, climate and target market.
  • Material reference: candidate code or reference sample, composition, weight and construction.
  • Feel and fit: surface, softness, stretch direction, compression preference and intended fit.
  • Colors and quantities: color references, units by style and color, and size breakdown.
  • Acceptance: checks, methods, criteria and supporting records required before approval.
  • Open decisions: alternatives to compare, missing specifications and target sample-review date.

Buyer questions

Activewear fabric FAQ

What information should a fabric brief include?

Include the product and activity, desired composition direction, weight, stretch and recovery, hand feel, compression direction, coverage, surface finish, target colors and any testing or certification requirements.

Does a higher fabric weight always mean better coverage?

No. Weight is only one variable. Construction, fiber, knit, color, stretch and garment fit also affect coverage, so assess the intended color in a finished sample under defined conditions.

What is the difference between stretch and recovery?

Stretch describes how far a fabric extends. Recovery describes how well it returns after extension. Both should be evaluated for the intended garment and use.

How should I compare two activewear fabric candidates?

Use one comparison sheet with each fabric code, composition, weight, construction, color, finish and the same review criteria. Record observations or test results under the same agreed conditions, then check the chosen fabric in the intended garment before approval.

How should activewear fabric be approved?

Record the fabric reference and specification, review a swatch, then evaluate the finished sample in the intended color, size and activity. Keep the approved reference with the project specification.

Describe the result you need from the fabric.

Send the product, activity, feel, stretch, coverage, color and any testing requirements with your project brief.
