Questions to resolve before shortlisting

This is a due-diligence checklist, not a claim that every item is automatically included by Glory Star Wear or another supplier.

  • Is the company identity consistent across quotation, invoice and official contact channels?
  • Who owns product decisions and who can approve commercial changes?
  • Does the supplier ask about activity, wearer, market and product requirements?
  • Which specification and sample version becomes the approval reference?
  • How are measurements, tolerances and construction requirements recorded?
  • How are fabric reference, color and finished-garment performance reviewed?
  • Which branding, labels, packaging and SKU requirements are included?
  • How do style, color, size and branding affect MOQ and quantity splits?
  • Which sample question is each sample intended to answer?
  • Which inspection stages, sample method and evidence are agreed?
  • What is included, excluded or estimated in the quotation?
  • Which payment, shipping, document and handoff assumptions apply?
  • How are deviations escalated, approved and retained in the record?
  • What response time and communication path will the project use?

Use one brief to compare scope

A fair quotation comparison starts before prices arrive. Give each candidate the same facts and mark the same unknowns.

Product and process

Company context, intended product, use, reference or tech pack, measurements, material, colors, sizes and timing.

Brand and pack-out

Artwork, placement, labels, packaging, SKU identification and approval responsibility.

Quality and release

Approved references, checkpoints, inspection scope, evidence, deviation handling and shipment authority.

Buyer questions

Choosing an activewear manufacturer FAQ

What should I verify before choosing a manufacturer?

Verify identity, location and official contact channels; compare product and customization fit; review sample and quality-control methods; obtain a scope-defined quotation; and confirm communication, change control, payment, shipping and documentation responsibilities.

How can I compare supplier quotations fairly?

Send the same dated brief to each supplier and compare the included style, material, colors, sizes, quantities, branding, labels, packaging, sample scope, quality-control scope, shipping assumptions, payment terms and exclusions.

Is the lowest quotation always the best choice?

No. A lower number may reflect a different product, material, service scope, quantity split, inspection plan, packaging or shipping assumption. Normalize the scope before comparing price and risk.

What should I learn from a sample?

A sample should answer defined questions about fit, measurements, material, construction, movement, coverage, branding and workmanship. Record the sample version and feedback against the same specification.
